New York — restless, brilliant, a world unto itself. Each season here feels like an act in an unforgettable play.

# Spring: The City Reawakens (March - May)
New York stretches after its winter slumber, and the parks burst into green and pink. Central Park's cherry blossoms, the re-opening of rooftop bars, and the buzz of the first street fairs mark spring.

Everything feels possible in a New York spring — and often is.

# Summer: Urban Heat and Energy (June - August)
In summer, New York sweats and sings. Festivals, concerts in the park, and movies under the stars fill every evening. The Fourth of July fireworks over the East River light up faces and skyscrapers alike.

Sure, it's humid — but there's nothing like a Manhattan night thick with dreams and jazz.

# Autumn: Storybook City (September - November)
Autumn in New York is mythic. Crisp air, blazing foliage in Prospect Park and Central Park, and a renewed energy as the city gears up for its cultural season. Thanksgiving parades, the magic of the pre-holiday buzz — it's cinematic in every way.

If you ever wanted to live inside a Woody Allen movie (minus the drama), autumn is your time.

# Winter: The Sparkle of Cold (December - February)
From the giant Christmas tree at Rockefeller Center to snowy strolls through the West Village, New York in winter is iconic. Skating rinks, hot pretzels, Broadway shows — the cold doesn't slow the city, it sharpens its charm.

Brave the chill, and you'll find New York's warmest heart hidden under its frosty coat.
